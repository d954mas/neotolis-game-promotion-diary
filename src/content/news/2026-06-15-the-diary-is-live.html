The diary is up and running. It's a self-tracking log for indie game devs, built around one stubborn question: which of the things I did actually moved my wishlists? Here's what it can already do today.

Log every promotion

Anything you do to push a game — a YouTube devlog, a Reddit thread, a Telegram post, a conference, a press email, a Discord drop — goes into one chronological feed. Months later it's all still there, in order, instead of scattered across ten dashboards and your memory.

Auto-import the busywork

Register a channel once and the diary pulls new posts in for you, with their numbers already attached — across six platforms: YouTube, Reddit, Telegram, Instagram, TikTok and X. It keeps re-reading the stats on a schedule, so views, likes and comments stay current without you touching a thing.

Wishlists on the same timeline

Tag a post with the game it promotes and it lands on that game's wishlist chart — your promo events marked right against the wishlist line. That's the whole point: you can finally see the spike next to the thing that caused it. (Steam wishlist numbers come in via the CSV export from your Steamworks page.)

Private, and yours

Everything is private by default and scoped to you — no public dashboards, no share links. It's free on the hosted instance (Google sign-in, no card), and it's open source under MIT if you'd rather self-host it on a small VPS. Same code, same behaviour either way.

More platforms and polish are on the way. If you try it, tell me what's missing.
